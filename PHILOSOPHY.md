# Voxprint AI Media Suite: Philosophy

*Complete, local, and built for the computer you have now: install it and start making.*

Voxprint is a set of desktop apps for making media with AI on your own computer: audiobooks, dubbed films, restored video, upscaled images, and books written from your own outline. Every app follows the same few ideas.

**Complete, with the decisions already made.** A good app should not hand you a box of parts. Like macOS or Windows, Voxprint picks the models, settings and pipeline for you and tunes them for your hardware, so you install it, press one button and get a finished result. You can still change things in Settings, but you shouldn't have to. The idea comes from "omakase", the chef's choice ([DHH, *Rails is omakase*](https://dhh.dk/2012/rails-is-omakase.html); [Omarchy](https://omarchy.org/)).

**Yours, like software used to be.** There is no account, no subscription, no online sign-in and no telemetry. Setup downloads the components and models once. After that, the work happens on your computer and your recordings, books and films never leave it. Any online feature, such as fetching subtitles, is optional and clearly marked.

**Facing forward.** A lot of developer time goes into supporting old systems. We'd rather spend ours on features and speed. Voxprint targets current Windows 11 and recent Linux releases and modern x86-64 CPUs. The apps require a supported NVIDIA GPU (RTX 40 series or newer) and won't start without one. When new hardware ships, we work to support it quickly. Support for older platforms ends after about two to three years, so the code stays lean and fast on the machines people are buying today.

**Open, in formats that last.** All Voxprint apps are free and open source under Apache-2.0. Models and voices keep their own licences, and each app lists them. By default we use modern open, royalty-free formats such as AV1, Opus, AVIF and JPEG XL. Older formats are still available as options where people need them.

**Honest about what works.** Voxprint is young and still in beta. We say what we have tested and on which hardware. When something has not been tested yet, we say so.
